Arbor Privacy Policy

Updated 8 November 2026

Arbor names and files your pictures on iPhone, iPad and Mac. This page says what Arbor does with your information, in plain words.

The short version

  • Arbor reads, names and files your pictures on your own devices.
  • Your pictures, their names, your Willows and Branches are never visible to us. On Plus, they're scrambled on your device before they're backed up, and nobody at Arbor can view them.
  • If you make an account, we keep a few basics: your name, your email, which sign-in you used, your plan, and how Arbor is being used (for example, when you last used it and how many pictures you have). Your birthday only if you choose to give it.
  • Paying for Plus goes through Apple. Arbor never sees your card.
  • No ads, no tracking, and nothing is sold.

Your pictures

Arbor only sees the pictures you give it: the ones you pick with Add Pictures, the ones you share to Arbor, and on the Mac the folders you choose. Reading the words in a picture, recognizing what's in it, naming it and filing it all happen on your own device.

When you save pictures back out of Arbor, they go where you choose: an album called "Arbor" in Photos, a folder in Files or iCloud Drive, or a folder on your Mac. Arbor only adds pictures to Photos; it never reads your whole photo library.

Your Arbor account

You can use Arbor without an account. To keep your pictures backed up and the same on all your devices (Plus), you sign in with Apple or with Google. Either one opens your Arbor account.

For your account we keep:

  • Your name, which you choose when you sign in (Apple or Google may fill it in for you).
  • Your email address. If you use Apple's Hide My Email, we only get Apple's private relay address.
  • Your birthday, only if you choose to give it. We use it to understand the ages of the people who use Arbor.
  • Whether you signed in with Apple or Google, whether you have Plus, and when you last signed in.
  • How Arbor is used: whether you use it on a Mac, an iPad or an iPhone and when you last used each, how many pictures you have and how much space they take, and how well Arbor's guesses are doing for you, as percentages and counts.

That's all. We never receive what your pictures show, their names, the words in them, your Willows, Branches, notes or anything else you put in Arbor. Only Arbor's own team can see your account basics, and we use them to look after Arbor and make it better.

Backup and syncing on Plus

On Plus, Arbor backs up your pictures and everything Arbor knows about them to Arbor's cloud, so a new or replaced device gets everything back when you sign in. Before anything leaves your device, Arbor scrambles it, and files are stored under random names. Arbor's cloud keeps the key that unscrambles them locked away and hands it only to your own signed-in devices. Nobody at Arbor can view your pictures, and there's no tool for doing so.

Arbor's cloud runs on Cloudflare, a company that stores the scrambled files and your account basics for us. Cloudflare can't read your pictures either.

On Basic, nothing is backed up and nothing syncs: each device keeps its own Arbor.

Signing in with Apple or Google

Apple or Google tells Arbor that it's really you, gives Arbor a number for you, and your email (and with Google, your name). Arbor never sees your Apple or Google password. To match an Apple sign-in and a Google sign-in that use the same email to one account, Arbor also keeps a one-way scrambled code of your email.

Web addresses in your pictures

When a picture shows a web address, Arbor may open that page to read its title or author, to help name the picture. That works like visiting the page in your browser: the website sees a normal visit from your device, including your internet address. The website never receives your picture.

The note reader download

Plus can read your written notes to Juno, Arbor's learning helper. That needs a download (about 1.7 GB), which you start yourself, from Hugging Face, a site that hosts these files. Only the download request is sent; nothing about you or your pictures. After the download, your notes are read on your own device.

Paying for Plus

Plus is a subscription through Apple, charged to the payment method on your Apple account. Apple handles the payment. Arbor only checks Apple's record of whether you have Plus, so it knows which features to open. You can manage or cancel it in Arbor's Upgrade screen, or in your Apple account's subscriptions.

What we don't do

  • We don't look at your pictures, and we have no way to.
  • We don't use advertising or tracking, and we don't sell or share your information with anyone.
  • We don't use outside analytics services inside Arbor.

Children

Arbor isn't made for children under 13, and we don't knowingly keep information about them. If you think a child under 13 has given us information, write to us and we'll delete it.

Deleting your information

Delete pictures in Arbor and they go to Arbor's Trash, then are removed. To remove your account and everything in Arbor's cloud, go to Settings, then Account & Storage, then Delete my account and data. That removes your account basics and every backed-up file at once, and cancels Arbor's Sign in with Apple. Pictures on your devices stay until you delete them or delete Arbor.

Changes to this page

If Arbor changes what it does with your information, this page changes first, with a new date at the top.
